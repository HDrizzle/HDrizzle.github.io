Mt Washington Hillclimb - Hadrian Ward

What is it

Photo 1: Me after the finish in 2024

Photo 2: View of the last bit of the road during the finish in 2026

Every year in August there is a bike race up the Mt Washington auto road. I first heard about it in 2023 from my neighbor Sid, who had done it ages ago. It is 7.6 miles and 4,690 feet of elevation gain. Once at the top everyone must wait for the race to finish to drive back down. Every rider must ride down in a car because riding down would overheat a bikes brakes. People would probably go too fast anyway and fly off the corners. It smells like brakes during the drive down and at the bottom.

My attempts

  • 2024: My first time, I was very conservative with my pacing which turned out perfect. My heartrate and vertical speed stayed extremely consistent and I finished in 1:17:13. It was an insane experience, weirdly calm compared to other bike races because everyone is going so slow. In normal bike races people clump up to draft off eachother which is more efficient. In this race there is no point in drafting which made it more of an individual event.
  • 2025: I didn't eat enough and went too fast initially with a time of 1:21:09.
  • 2026: First time with my KOM Timer (more later), I finished in 1:17:17 so 4 secounds too slow I was very pissed at myself. I did eat enough though. NOTE FOR THE FUTURE: When hillclimbing my back often hurts which makes me slow down. However I need to concentrate on the fact that my back hurting does not mean that my legs are also hurting, I should keep going fast and Only pay attention to pain in my legs and not pain in general.

Bike setup

Normal road gears aren't low enough so every year I put mountain bike gears on the road bike. In 2026 I am even ditching the 2nd front sprocket to make it a 1-by.

  • Front gear: Use only the smallest of the 3 MTB gears
  • Front shifter: None
  • Back gears: The bigger MTB cassette also fits right on the road bike wheel.
  • Back shifter: Use the shifter with the larger distance between the 2 jockey wheels. NOTE: Even though the road and MTB gear spacing in the cassette is different, the shifters move differently with the same cable movement so the MTB cassette/shifter combination is still indexed properly.
