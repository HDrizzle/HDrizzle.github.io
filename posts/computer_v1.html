8 Bit Computer - Hadrian Ward

Fantasy

When I was little (around 11) my parents bought me a book, Practical Electronics For Inventors that I real alot from. At the time i didn't know much math and what I now know to be integrals were scary symbols (they are no less scary of course). The one topic that made alot of sense was digital logic and I quickly started thinking about how to make a computer. In middle school I had two versions of block diagrams which were very fantastical, as in not much detail, many details not explained. The one idea I came up with that I did eventually use was the concept of a "baton" clock signal that gets passed around the computer as each part does its thing.

Actual plans

After having not thought about it for awhile I decided winter of junior year of high school (early 2024) that I wanted to design a computer. The goal was to be able to run tetris on it. I talked to Dr Hamlin - a teacher at MSSM - about it and he gave me a book about computer architecture. I read alot of the book and got some ideas from it which I don't remember exactly. I realized that I would need to create timing diagrams to describe how all the pieces communicated with eachother.

Basic architecture

Before I did anything else I needed a rough block diagram and the instruction format. I settled on a the block diagram shown below which used 16-bit instructions and 8-bit data words. The data and program memories are seperated which resembles Harvard architecture. All data is moved around on the 8-bit bus between the memory, ALU, IO, etc.

Design software

  • Wavedrom, a simple timing waveform editor / renderer.
  • Circuitverse, a digital circuit simulator, which I later based my own program Rusty Logic on.
  • KiCad, Schematic and PCB software.
