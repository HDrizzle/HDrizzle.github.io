Antenna configurations

An antenna creates or rcieves an alternating electric field. I have experimented with dipoles and quarter-wave-vertical-ground-planes. A vertical-gound-plane resembles a dipole where half the dipole is vertical, and the other half of the dipole is "mirrored" in an infinite conducting plane (the ground plane). Because infinite conducting planes don't exist, a few radial wires spread across the ground all connected as the other side of the dipole also works. People more experienced than me have experimented with vertical wire length, radial wire length, and number of radial wires. I have just used whatever wire I have from the piles of junk.

Loading coil

The lowest commonly used Ham Band is 160 meters WL, or 40 meters quarter-wave-vertical-ground-plane which is high to climb in a tree. My point is that constructing an antenna with the ideal geometry is sometimes difficult. To make a much shorter wire resonate at longer wavelengths an inductor can be placed before it at the feedpoint (or anywhere along the wire). A loading coil works by canceling out the capacitance of an antenna that is too short, making it resonate at a lower frequency. The loading coil I built has a wiper for adjusting the inductance and therefore the resonant frequency.
