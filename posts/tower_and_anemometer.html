Tower & Anemometer - Hadrian Ward

Origin

Way back, me (and somewhat my brother) had an idea to build something called The Structure. Mom suggested using compressed-earth-bricks. Instead I ended up building a square tower from 2" X 2" wood pieces leftover from something.

Improvements, Setbacks

It reached 3 stories and at some point had a swing and an elevator with a box and a winch to haul tools up to the top. I don't remember how many times but it got blown down many times. I remember always looking out the window after a big storm to check that it was still up. At some point I put 4 ropes in it to hold it up, although the wind still blew the stakes out multiple times. I then put heavy rocks in the bottom on the "ground floor" which also didn't work. I remember going out in the wind and rain to move the heaviest rocks to whatever side the wind was comming from.

At some point the entire tower was moved behind the shop away from the tree cluster. After this I had made it robust enough that it never blew down again.

Anemometer

At some point I decided to make a windmill style anemometer to put on the tower. I knew that the cup-wheel-and-weathervane style would be easier but not as cool. Just like the tower itself the anemometer went through many iterations to survive the wind. The first version had a mechanical lever attached to the shaft of the blades that would be pulled back then hit the wood every rotation. It was loud and stupid but I was able to pick up the vibrations on the stationary support stick.

I then figured out I could have a vertical metal rod that the windmill assembly rotated on. There were 2 magnets (so it stays balanced) with the poles aligned glued on opposite sides of the blade shaft. The rotating magnets exposed alternating poles to the vertical rod which "carries" the field down to the stationary mount with a pickup coil. Later to get the wind direction I had a seperate magnet/sensor further down the rod. At this point the tower had been moved behind the shop away from the trees.
